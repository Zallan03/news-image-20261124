## 1. Three oil tankers hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said three oil tankers
suffered severe explosions and fires after striking naval mines in
the Strait of Hormuz. The tankers had entered a restricted zone east
of the strait, according to Iranian state media. The Strait of Hormuz
carries about a fifth of the world's traded oil, so the incident
immediately raised shipping and energy-market tension.

- First reported: **24 November 2026, ~00:15 UTC** (IRGC statement)
- Sources: Al Jazeera (Iran war live, 24 Nov 2026); IRNA via

## 2. US sanctions Iran's shadow banking networks

The US Department of the Treasury, under Operation Economic Outcast,
sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front
companies and key personnel, aiming to disrupt the shadow banking
networks Tehran uses to move billions of dollars and earn foreign
currency. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said: "Iran's economy is
underwater, and its leaders are panicking."

- Announced: **Monday 23 November 2026** (Washington), reported
  24 November in Asia (ANI dateline 24 Nov)
- Sources: US Department of the Treasury,
  https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0699/ ;
  Al Jazeera and Reuters reporting of the announcement, 23 Nov 2026

## 3. Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO

Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, effective
immediately, an extraordinary reversal under pressure from US
President Donald Trump, after Huang resigned on 12 November
following his admission of an extramarital affair. Trump said he
"made the deal". Nvidia shares surged more than 31% in premarket
trading on Monday.

- Announced: **Monday 23 November 2026**
- Sources: Reuters,
  https://www.reuters.com/technology/nvidia-reinstates-jensen-huang-ceo-2026-11-23/ ;
  BBC News and CNN, 23 Nov 2026

## 4. FIFA names the 2031 and 2035 Women's World Cup hosts

FIFA confirmed the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica as
hosts of the 2031 Women's World Cup — the first edition with 48
teams — and England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales as hosts
of the 2035 edition, the first Women's World Cup staged in the UK.
Both bids were approved by acclamation at FIFA's Extraordinary
Congress.

- Announced: **Monday 23 November 2026** (Zurich)
- Sources: FIFA,

  https://www.reuters.com/sports/soccer/us-mexico-costa-rica-jamaica-awarded-2031-womens-world-cup-uk-host-2035-edition-2026-11-23/
